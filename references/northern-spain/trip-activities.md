# Family Trip — Destinations & Things to Do

*Companion to the logistics document. Route order: Madrid → Oviedo → Covadonga (transfer day) → western Cantabria → Bilbao.*

*Food philosophy for the whole trip: unpretentious classics and great ingredients, zero Michelin-chasing. Northern Spain is arguably the best place in Europe for exactly that. Deliberately light on museums too — only the Prado and the Guggenheim get timed tickets; everywhere else, the town is the attraction.*

---

## Madrid (3 nights — Fri, Sat, Sun)

Nobody in the family has been. The plan is deliberately light: one museum done well, then streets, park, market, and long tapas evenings.

- **The Prado — the one Madrid museum.** Timed entry at opening, and a **90-minute highlights visit only**: Velázquez's *Las Meninas*, Goya (the majas, the black paintings), Bosch's *Garden of Earthly Delights* — then out before fatigue sets in. (Reina Sofía and the Royal Palace interior are deliberately cut; the palace exterior and the Almudena viewpoint are a nice stroll-past.)
- **Retiro Park** — the Crystal Palace, the boating lake, evening paseo with the locals.
- **Old-town wander** — Plaza Mayor → Mercado de San Miguel (a look, maybe one bite) → the lanes of La Latina.
- **Sobrino de Botín — the one booked table.** The world's oldest continuously operating restaurant (1725): wood-fired roast suckling pig and lamb. Classic-dish institution, not a scene.
- **Tapas evenings** — Cava Baja street in La Latina: tortilla, gambas al ajillo, jamón from a proper taberna. **Churros con chocolate at San Ginés** late or early.
- **Sunday morning: El Rastro** flea market in La Latina, vermouth at the surrounding bars after (the local Sunday ritual) — if we're there on a Sunday, which the Friday arrival guarantees.
- **Segovia — optional, game-time decision.** ~30 min by AVE from Chamartín: the Roman aqueduct, the Alcázar, wood-roasted *cochinillo* for lunch. Decided on the ground by energy and weather; it becomes a committed day if the flex night goes to Madrid.

## Oviedo (2 nights)

Asturias' elegant little capital: a polished medieval old town and the spiritual home of Spanish cider. **No museums here** — the town, the market, and the cider culture are the program.

- **Calle Gascona, "the cider boulevard"** — sidrerías where waiters pour cider from above their heads (*escanciar*). Order *fabada asturiana*, *cachopo*, and keep the cider coming. The anti-Michelin dinner of the trip.
- **The old town** — pedestrianized lanes, the cathedral square, Plaza del Fontán.
- **Mercado El Fontán** — Asturian cheeses (Cabrales!), chorizo, picnic supplies for the road-trip days.
- **The pre-Romanesque churches on Monte Naranco** — Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo, UNESCO-listed 9th-century royal buildings from the mountain kingdom that started the Reconquista. 10 min by taxi, city panorama included.

## The Covadonga day (transfer day, Oviedo → Cantabria)

The trip's headline mountain visit, folded into the move east. Early start; luggage loaded in Oviedo and the trunk stays shut until the hotel; daypacks carry the valuables.

- **Lakes of Covadonga first** (morning): the Alsa shuttle from Cangas de Onís bus station up to lakes Enol and Ercina at ~1,100 m — green glacial water, grazing cows, the Picos all around. Short optional strolls between viewpoints; nothing strenuous. (Road closed to private cars June–mid-Oct; shuttle booked online in advance.)
- **The basilica and Holy Cave after** — the pink basilica in its mountain amphitheater and the shrine in the cliff where, tradition says, the Reconquista began.
- **Lunch in Cangas de Onís** — the medieval-looking "Roman bridge" with the Victory Cross hanging beneath it is the postcard.
- **Driving east afterward, optional stop if energy allows:** **Llanes** (fishing-town old quarter and port), **Playa de Gulpiyuri** (the famous inland beach — a sinkhole meadow that fills with sea through a cave; short flat walk, best near high tide — hold it loosely, it underwhelms at low tide), or the **San Roque viewpoint above Lastres** (ten minutes, the classic cliff-village panorama).
- **Weather rule:** bad morning = skip the lakes (no shuttle into cloud); see the basilica briefly or push on to the coast stops. Fuente Dé later in the week is the mountain backup.

## Western Cantabria — 4 nights (Comillas / San Vicente de la Barquera / Santillana del Mar)

Three of northern Spain's prettiest small towns, 10–20 minutes apart — sea views from Comillas or San Vicente, medieval perfection at Santillana. Four nights = arrival evening + three real days, with built-in slack.

- **Coast day:**
  - **Santillana del Mar** — "the most beautiful village in Spain": intact medieval lanes, best early or in the evening when the day-trippers leave.
  - **Comillas** — **El Capricho**, the young Gaudí's sunflower-tiled villa (one of only three Gaudí works outside Catalonia), plus a proper beach and a handsome old town.
  - **Altamira museum — optional:** the faithful replica of the "Sistine Chapel of cave art" (15,000-year-old bison). Book if the group wants it; skip guilt-free.
- **Weather-flex day:** **Fuente Dé + Potes** if the morning webcam is clear — cable car 750 vertical meters up the rock face to 1,823 m, easy strolling on top, the La Hermida gorge road there and back, and Potes (stone bridges, *cocido lebaniego*, a sip of *orujo*) on the way home. **Santander if cloudy** — Magdalena peninsula, El Sardinero beach promenade, Centro Botín, Mercado de la Esperanza.
- **Spare day:** beach time, **Santoña** (the anchovy capital — shops and pintxos), San Vicente's harbor front, or whichever day above got weathered out.
- Eat: Santoña anchovies, *rabas* (fried squid), hake and sea bream off the boats, *sobaos* and *quesada pasiega* for the road.

## Bilbao — 3 nights (the finale)

A former industrial city reborn around one building, with the best casual eating of the trip. Three nights = two city evenings + a full Basque coast day, all from one hotel.

- **The Guggenheim** — the one full museum of the north: Gehry's titanium ship, Serra's *The Matter of Time*, the Puppy outside. Timed entry booked ahead.
- **Casco Viejo pintxos.** The Siete Calles and the arcaded Plaza Nueva: counter-top grazing, bar to bar. Three evenings cover the range: one pintxos night, one casual harbor night (Santurtzi/Algorta), one booked asador splurge.
- **Mercado de la Ribera** — Europe's largest covered market, riverside, gastro-bars for lunch.
- **Artxanda funicular** — three minutes up to the balcony view over the city; golden hour with the parents.
- **Basque coast day — the Urdaibai loop (Monday, while the Guggenheim is closed):**
  - **Gaztelugatxe** — the hermitage islet and its winding stone causeway; free timed permit in season; ~240 steps each way, but the clifftop viewpoint needs none.
  - **Bermeo** — a real working fishing port: simple portside lunch among the fishermen.
  - **Gernika** — the Basque assembly house and its oak, and, on Mondays, the famous **market day** — the living version of the market Picasso's painting depicts.
  - All within 30–45 min of Bilbao on the near coast; one coherent loop, no motorway marathon.
- **The seafood plan, two registers:**
  - **Casual:** grilled sardines on the harbor at **Santurtzi**, or *rabas* and anchovies eaten on the stone steps of **Algorta's Puerto Viejo** — both on the metro, both the plastic-stools-spirit answer to how this family loves to eat. En route between them: the **Vizcaya Bridge**, the world's first transporter bridge (UNESCO, 1893) — cross the river hanging from its gondola for under a euro on the way to dinner.
  - **Splurge:** one booked dinner at a **traditional Bilbao asador/fish house** — whole wild turbot or *besugo* over coals, the same fish from the same auctions as Getaria's famous grills; only the harbor setting is missing.
- **If the flex night lands in Bilbao (current lean):** the bonus day is **Getaria + Zumaia** — the harbor grill row, txakoli on the hill above it, and the flysch cliffs 15 minutes away. Getaria is deliberately *not* load-bearing: it's the encore, not the show.
- Final morning: drive to the airport, drop the car, fly.

## How we eat (the through-line)

The trip is built around one idea at the table: **classic dishes, great ingredients, zero pretense.** Two food cultures share one sea on this route: **Cantabria cooks it simply** (port-town terraces, fish a la plancha, paper tablecloths, no reservations), and **the Basques cook it proudly** (pintxos counters, sauce craft, coal-fire grills). We get both, in that order.

- **Madrid:** tapas bars and one 300-year-old roast house (Botín) — tortilla, jamón, roast lamb, churros at midnight.
- **(Optional) Segovia:** wood-roasted *cochinillo* in the aqueduct's shadow.
- **Oviedo:** cider poured from arm's length above the glass, *fabada*, *cachopo* — dinner on a street built entirely of cider houses.
- **Cantabria — the casual-seafood heart of the trip:** harbor-front dinners in a working fishing port (fresh hake, sea bream, *rabas*, September's *bonito*), the world's best anchovies in Santoña, *sobaos* and *quesada* from village bakeries — all walk-in, all unceremonious.
- **Potes (weather permitting):** *cocido lebaniego* — the highland chickpea stew — and a sip of *orujo*.
- **Bilbao, two registers:** pintxos counters and the Ribera market by day; grilled sardines at Santurtzi's harbor or anchovies on Algorta's old-port steps for the casual night; and **one booked splurge at a traditional asador** — whole wild turbot over coals, Getaria-grade fish without leaving the city.
- **Getaria (only if the extra Bilbao night happens):** the encore — the harbor grill row itself, txakoli grown on the hill above it.

Not one tasting menu in twelve nights. Reservations exist only where walk-ins fail: Botín and the Bilbao asador (plus Getaria, if the encore happens).

---

## The trip in one line

One museum, one palace skipped, a fast train under the mountains, a shuttle to two glacial lakes, four slow nights on the storybook coast, and a titanium ship at the end — with the best unpretentious eating in Europe the whole way.

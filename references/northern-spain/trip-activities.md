# Family Trip — Destinations & Things to Do

*Companion to the logistics document. Same route order: Madrid → Oviedo → eastern Asturias (Llanes) → western Cantabria → Bilbao.*

*Food philosophy for the whole trip: unpretentious classics and great ingredients, zero Michelin-chasing. Northern Spain is arguably the best place in Europe for exactly that — cider houses, fishing-port grills, market bars, and dishes that haven't changed in a century.*

---

## Madrid (3 nights — Fri, Sat, Sun)

Nobody in the family has been. The plan: the two great museums, the old town on foot, and long tapas evenings — plus one fast-train day trip.

- **The Prado** — one of the top three painting museums on earth: Velázquez's *Las Meninas*, Goya's black paintings, Bosch's *Garden of Earthly Delights*. Book timed entry; go at opening. (The free evening hours exist but are the most crowded slot — skip them.)
- **The Reina Sofía** — for one painting above all: Picasso's *Guernica* — which lands differently after you've visited Gernika itself at the end of this trip. A neat full-circle the family can enjoy.
- **Retiro Park** — the city's garden: the Crystal Palace, the boating lake, evening paseo with the locals.
- **Royal Palace** — Europe's largest functioning royal palace; book timed entry. Pair with the Almudena cathedral and the Sabatini gardens viewpoint.
- **Old-town wander** — Plaza Mayor → Mercado de San Miguel (look, maybe one bite — it's touristy) → the lanes of La Latina.
- **Tapas evenings** — Cava Baja street in La Latina is the classic crawl; sit-down alternative any night: patatas bravas, gambas al ajillo, tortilla, jamón from a proper *taberna*.
- **Sobrino de Botín** (optional, but very much this family's style): the world's oldest continuously operating restaurant (1725), wood-fired roast suckling pig and lamb — a classic-dish institution, not a scene. Book ahead.
- **Churros con chocolate at San Ginés** — the traditional late-night (or breakfast) stop.
- **Sunday morning: El Rastro** — the sprawling flea market in La Latina, then vermouth at the surrounding bars (the local Sunday ritual).
- **Day trip by AVE: Segovia** (~30 min each way from Chamartín) — the intact Roman aqueduct, the fairy-tale Alcázar, and lunch on the town's famous wood-roasted *cochinillo* (suckling pig). Gentle, spectacular, and the food fits the family brief exactly. (Alternative: Toledo, ~35 min — bigger, hillier, hotter; Segovia is the easier day with parents.)

## Oviedo (2 nights)

Asturias' elegant little capital: a polished medieval old town, and the spiritual home of Spanish cider.

- **Calle Gascona, "the cider boulevard"** — a street of *sidrerías* where waiters pour cider from above their heads (*escanciar*) to wake it up. Order *fabada asturiana* (the mighty bean-and-sausage stew), *cachopo* (two veal escalopes stuffed with ham and cheese), and just keep the cider coming. This is the anti-Michelin meal of the trip.
- **The old town** — pedestrianized lanes, the Cathedral (with the Cámara Santa, a UNESCO-listed 9th-century chapel), Plaza del Fontán and its market.
- **Mercado El Fontán** — the covered food market: Asturian cheeses (Cabrales!), chorizo, the makings of road-trip picnics.
- **The pre-Romanesque churches on Monte Naranco** — Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo, UNESCO-listed 9th-century royal buildings from the mountain kingdom that started the Reconquista — unique in Europe, 10 min by taxi, with a city panorama thrown in.
- Gentle city otherwise: clean, walkable, unhurried — a deliberate decompression stop between Madrid and the road trip.

## Eastern Asturias — Llanes base (2 nights)

Fishing town on a coastline of cliffs and coves, with the Picos de Europa as a permanent backdrop.

- **The Covadonga day (committed):** the basilica in its mountain amphitheater, the Santa Cueva shrine in the cliff, then up the famous road to the **Lakes of Covadonga** (Enol and Ercina, ~1,100 m) — green glacial lakes, grazing cows, peaks all around. Short optional strolls between viewpoints; nothing strenuous. ⚠️ In high season private cars are banned from the lakes road during the day and a shuttle bus runs from Cangas de Onís — check whether restrictions still apply on our dates (early-September weekends likely; weekdays maybe not). Cangas de Onís itself (the Roman-looking "Puente Romano" with the Victory Cross) is the natural coffee/lunch stop.
- **Llanes town** — the old quarter and port, the Paseo de San Pedro clifftop walk (flat, grassy, sea on one side and Picos on the other — made for a parents' evening stroll), the painted harbor "Cubes of Memory."
- **Playa de Gulpiyuri** — the famous *inland* beach: a sinkhole meadow 100 m from the sea that fills with tidal water through a cave. Short flat walk from parking; go near high tide or it's a sandpit.
- **Ribadesella** (if not already stopped on the drive in) — handsome river-mouth town; the **Tito Bustillo cave** has world-class paleolithic art (very limited daily tickets, book well ahead — or save cave art for Altamira's museum in Cantabria).
- Eat: seafood by the port, more cider — this coast is where the sidrería habit sticks.

## Western Cantabria — Comillas / Santillana del Mar base (3 nights)

Two of northern Spain's prettiest small towns, 15 minutes apart, with the coast on one side and the Picos on the other. Three nights = arrival day + a coast day + the weather-flex mountain day.

- **The coast day:**
  - **Santillana del Mar** — "the most beautiful village in Spain" per generations of guidebooks: an intact medieval town of stone lanes and balconied houses (yes, touristy — go early or in the evening when the day-trippers leave and we're sleeping next door).
  - **The Altamira Museum** — the faithful replica (*Neocueva*) of the "Sistine Chapel of cave art," 15,000-year-old bison painted on the ceiling. The replica is the visit — entry to the real cave is a tiny weekly lottery. Book museum tickets ahead.
  - **Comillas** — **El Capricho**, the young Gaudí's sunflower-tiled villa (one of only three Gaudí buildings outside Catalonia), plus the Sobrellano palace and a proper beach.
- **The weather-flex mountain day — Fuente Dé (go if the morning is clear):** drive the **La Hermida gorge** into the Liébana valley (~1¼ h), ride the **cable car** 750 vertical meters up the rock face to 1,823 m, stroll the top plateau *inside* the central massif — the one high-alpine moment of the trip, zero fitness required. On the way back: **Potes**, the medieval mountain town — stone bridges, *cocido lebaniego* (chickpea highland stew), a sip of *orujo*. Go early to beat cable-car queues.
  - **If the weather says no:** swap in **Santander** (the Palacio de la Magdalena peninsula, El Sardinero beach promenade, the Centro Botín gallery, and the Mercado de la Esperanza food market) — a fine big-town day that needs no sunshine. Or simply more coast: **Santoña** and its anchovy shops.
- Eat: Cantabrian anchovies (the best in the world, from Santoña), *rabas* (fried squid), sea bream and hake, *sobaos* and *quesada pasiega* (the local butter-cake and cheesecake) for the road.

## Bilbao (2 nights) — plus the Basque-coast day

A former industrial city reborn around one building, with the best casual eating of the trip and the car kept for one last coastal outing.

- **The Guggenheim** — Gehry's titanium ship, worth the trip for the building alone (plus Serra's *The Matter of Time* inside, the Puppy and the spider outside). Book timed entry.
- **The Casco Viejo (old town)** — the "Siete Calles," the arcaded Plaza Nueva ringed with pintxos bars: this is the pintxos experience without San Sebastián's crush — grazing dinner both nights is a legitimate plan.
- **Mercado de la Ribera** — Europe's largest covered market, riverside, with gastro-bars downstairs for lunch.
- **Artxanda funicular** — three minutes up to the balcony view over the whole city; flat strolling at the top. Ideal with parents at golden hour.
- **The Basque-coast day (with the car):**
  - **Getaria** (~1 h east) — tiny fishing town where whole wild turbot and bream are **grilled over coals outdoors** at the harbor grill houses, with *txakoli* wine from the hillside above. Arguably the single most "this family's style" meal in Spain. Book the table ahead; walk the harbor and the mouse-shaped headland after.
  - On the way back, choose one: **Gaztelugatxe** — the hermitage on its stone causeway (the *Game of Thrones* one). ⚠️ Free timed permit required in season, and it's ~240 steps each way — parents can enjoy the clifftop viewpoint without descending. Or **Gernika** — the Basque assembly house and its oak, and the Picasso connection back to the Reina Sofía on day two. Or **Zumaia's flysch cliffs** — tilted rock strata like the pages of a book.
- Final morning: drive to the airport, drop the car, fly.

## The trip in one line

A weekend of art and tapas in the capital → a fast train under the mountains to cider country → a slow drive along Spain's greenest coast with the Picos de Europa out one window and the sea out the other → and a finale of pintxos, harbor-grilled fish, and Gehry's titanium ship in Bilbao.

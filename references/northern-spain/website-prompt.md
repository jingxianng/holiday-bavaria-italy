# Prompt: Trip-Planning Website Generator — Northern Spain

Reference the two attached documents (`trip-logistics.md` and `trip-activities.md`) for more information when building the website.

---

I'm planning a multi-generational family trip through Madrid and "Green Spain" (Asturias, Cantabria, the Basque Country) in early September, and I've attached two documents: one covering all logistics (flights, the fast train, the one-way rental car, nights per stop) and one covering the destinations and activities. Please build a single-page trip-planning website from them.

**Before building anything: review both documents and check their assumptions.** Flag anything that looks inconsistent, outdated, or worth verifying (train times, drive times, access restrictions like the Covadonga lakes shuttle, booking lead times, etc.) and list your findings first. Where the documents say "verify," the site should say "we'll confirm the details" rather than inventing specifics. Only after that review, build the site.

**Audience:** primarily my parents (60s–70s, flying from Singapore). Nobody in the family has been to Madrid or northern Spain, so the whole route is new — but don't oversell it as exotic; the tone is "this will be beautiful, delicious, and easy." The family food philosophy matters and should color the food content: unpretentious classics and great ingredients, zero Michelin-chasing — cider houses, fishing-port grills, market bars. We deliberately skipped San Sebastián (overtouristed) in favor of quieter places that eat just as well; the site shouldn't dwell on this, but the Getaria grill day and Bilbao's pintxos are the Basque food flags. Secondary: reassure everyone that the driving is short and gentle (three hops of ~1–1.5 h) and that mountain days need no fitness (cable car, short strolls). The site's job is buy-in: get everyone excited and confident that the logistics are smooth and taken care of. Warm and reassuring in tone, not a booking tool. English, generous font sizes and high contrast, fully mobile-friendly (they'll view it on phones).

**Structure:**
1. A hero section with the trip's one-line arc and a route overview — an elegant step-by-step visual of the stops (Madrid 3 → Oviedo 2 → western Cantabria 4 → Bilbao 3, with Covadonga as the en-route highlight of the transfer day) with nights at each. No map needed.
2. A "how we'll travel" section that reframes the logistics as comfort: no car at all until Oviedo (city days + one fast train under the mountains), then one car, one big well-planned transfer day (Covadonga by shuttle, luggage loaded once), a 4-night base, one short hop to Bilbao, and an airport drop on the way out. A simple visual day-by-day timeline works well here. Mention the weather-flex rules as a feature (Covadonga lakes skipped if the morning is bad; Fuente Dé decided at breakfast) and the flex-night option (Madrid or Bilbao +1, Cantabria −1) as still open.
3. One section per destination, in route order, drawing from the activities document: a short evocative intro, then highlights as scannable cards. The Covadonga transfer day deserves its own section between Oviedo and Cantabria. Only two full museums exist on this trip (Prado 90-minute highlights, Guggenheim) — the site should present the light-museum plan as a deliberate feature. Include a photo for each destination (royalty-free Wikimedia Commons images downloaded locally with a credits section, as on the Alps site) — key subjects: the Lakes of Covadonga, Plaza Mayor, the Segovia aqueduct, cider pouring in Oviedo, the Covadonga basilica, Gulpiyuri, El Capricho, Santillana, Fuente Dé, the Guggenheim, Getaria's harbor, Gaztelugatxe.
4. A "how we'll eat" section (standalone or woven into the flow — builder's choice, but it must exist): the family food philosophy and the food through-line of the route, from tapas and Botín to cider houses, Santoña anchovies, pintxos, and the Getaria grill.
5. A short FAQ addressing likely concerns: How much walking? (moderate, own pace, shuttle and cable car do the climbing) Is the driving hard? (one big transfer day, then short hops) Language/money? (English fine in cities, cards everywhere) September weather? (the driest window Green Spain offers, but pack a light rain layer — and both mountain moments flex around it) What about the separate flight tickets home? (generous connection built in) Is the plan final? (the flex-night option, bookings held refundable).
6. External links where natural: Renfe, the Prado, the Guggenheim, Gaztelugatxe permits — opened in new tabs.

**Design:** same design language as the Alps site (`site/alps-2027/index.html`): cream/ink/alpine palette, serif headings, card grid, sticky section nav, lightbox, mobile-first. Everything self-contained in one HTML file with local images, relative paths, and `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`.

Do not invent train times, prices, or hotel names beyond what the documents contain. Accuracy over embellishment.

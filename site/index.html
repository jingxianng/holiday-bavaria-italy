How we'll travel: one car, then the world's most scenic trains

The whole trip is built so that no travel day feels like a travel day. We keep a car only for the Bavarian leg, where it's genuinely useful — and from the moment we drop it at Lindau, nobody in the family drives again. Everything after Germany is trains and lake ferries, with reserved seats and our bags beside us.

Every hop is a half-day or less — except two long rail days that are the point, not the price: the Bernina crossing of the Alps, and the fast, comfortable run from the lake to Venice. Sleep in the same bed four nights running at Lake Como, three in Venice. This is a slow trip on purpose.

Munich → Rettenberg

~2 hours by car

Pick up the rental in Munich and roll south-west into the Allgäu. An easy motorway drive — or the slower, prettier Alpenstraße if we feel like it.

Rettenberg — 2–3 nights with Dr. H

Family time, village time, mountain time. The car stays with us for day trips.

Rettenberg → Lindau → Chur

~50 min drive + ~2 h train

A morning in Lindau's old harbor town on Lake Constance, drop the car for good, then a lovely train ride into Switzerland down the Rhine valley. Exact departure to be confirmed once the car return is booked.

Chur — 1 night

An evening stroll and a good dinner in Switzerland's oldest town — so we board the great train fresh the next morning, in morning light.

The Bernina Railway: Chur → Tirano → Varenna

Morning panoramic train + short lunch stop + ~1¼ h regional

The headline day. Reserved panoramic seats over the highest rail crossing of the Alps, lunch in Italy where the two stations face each other across a little square, then an easy lakeside train to Varenna by mid-afternoon.

Varenna, Lake Como — 4 nights

The slow middle of the trip. Ferries, gardens, long lunches.

Varenna → Venice

~4 hours door to door

Regional train along the lake to Milan, one escalator-and-platform change, then the fast Frecciarossa — about 2½ comfortable reserved-seat hours — arriving at a station that opens directly onto the Grand Canal.

Venice — 3 nights

Early mornings and late evenings in the city, after the day-trippers leave.

Venice → Milan → home

~2½ h train, then a calm final morning

We never chain a train straight into a long-haul flight: Milan gets its own farewell evening, and the airport express is a simple 50-minute ride the next day.

Booking notes (the boring bits, handled): the car is a domestic German one-way (Munich → Lindau) — the cheap kind; panoramic-train seats get reserved the moment dates are fixed, since September fills up; Swiss tickets via SBB, Italian via Trenitalia. Where exact times matter — the Lindau car return, the morning train — we'll confirm them before anything is booked. Nothing here requires anyone else to plan anything.

Munich — the gentle landing

An optional first night to shake off the flights before the mountains. No sightseeing agenda — just the pleasant, familiar rhythm of a city Pa knows well.

Marienplatz & Viktualienmarkt

The old-town heart and the food market next door — a perfect first-morning wander before we pick up the car. The Glockenspiel plays at 11:00 and 12:00.

A classic beer-hall evening

Hofbräuhaus for the famous one, Augustiner Bräustuben for the local's choice. Pretzels, roast pork, litre steins.

English Garden

If energy allows: a stroll to watch the river surfers on the Eisbach wave.

Rettenberg & the Allgäu — family days in dairy country

Green foothills, onion-domed churches, and the Alps rising straight ahead — and at the centre of it, two or three days staying with Dr. H in the village. This leg isn't about ticking sights; it's about porch mornings, mountain air, and catching up with old friends.

In the village

Germany's southernmost brewery village

Rettenberg is home to Zötler — which claims to be Germany's oldest family-run brewery, brewing since 1447 — and Engelbräu. Brewery tours and beer gardens on the doorstep.

In the village

Grünten & the cheese dairies

Easy walks and viewpoints on "the guardian of the Allgäu," and alpine dairies selling Bergkäse straight from the source.

For X & E · e-bikes

The Iller valley ride

The signature day: ~50 km of flat valley path to Oberstdorf and back, mountains in view the whole way — with the Breitachklamm gorge walk or a cable car up the Nebelhorn at the turnaround. E-bikes rent locally.

For X & E · e-bikes

Lakes & valleys

A rolling lakes day (Großer Alpsee, Niedersonthofener See — still swimmable in early September), or the climb into Kleinwalsertal, a dead-end Austrian valley reachable only from Germany.

Day trip by car

Neuschwanstein & the Wieskirche

The fairy-tale castle (~1¼ h): the Marienbrücke viewpoint is the real highlight, with lunch in pretty Füssen — and the UNESCO rococo Wieskirche, standing alone in its meadow, as the natural add-on. Interior tickets get booked well in advance; the views need no ticket at all.

Day trip by car

Lindau — or Bregenz

Lindau's historic harbor island on Lake Constance is where we drop the car anyway — we'll arrive early and enjoy it. Alternative outing: the Pfänder cable car above Bregenz for the lake panorama.

Chur — one night in Switzerland's oldest town

Five thousand years of settlement, tucked into the Rhine valley. We sleep here for one clever reason: the great panoramic train leaves in the morning — so we cross the Alps fresh, in morning light, instead of tired at the end of a ten-hour slog.

The car-free Altstadt

An evening stroll through lanes, courtyards, and squares like the Arcas — five millennia of town underfoot.

The cathedral on the hill

The 12th-century cathedral and the bishop's court district above the rooftops — a ten-minute climb, worth every step.

A Graubünden dinner

Capuns, pizzokel, Bündnerfleisch — the mountain canton's specialties, then an early night.

The headline day

The Bernina Railway — the crossing

A UNESCO World Heritage line and the highest rail crossing of the Alps: glaciers, spiral viaducts and turquoise lakes, watched from a reserved panoramic seat with coffee in hand. This is a travel day the way a concert is a commute.

Landwasser Viaduct & the Albula spirals

The iconic curved bridge straight into a cliff-face tunnel, then a line that corkscrews inside the mountain to gain height.

Glaciers & the summit

The Morteratsch Glacier and the Bernina massif — permanent snow in view even in September — then the 2,253 m summit at Ospizio Bernina beside the milky-turquoise Lago Bianco.

Down into Italy

The balcony view from Alp Grüm, the famous Brusio circular viaduct where the train loops over itself, and arrival among the vineyards in Tirano — where lunch means pizzoccheri, the Valtellina's buckwheat pasta.

Then a short lakeside hop

From Tirano, an easy regional train (~1¼ h) along Lake Como's eastern shore brings us to Varenna by mid-afternoon. Morning departure from Chur — historically around 8:15, arriving Tirano around a quarter to one; we'll confirm the exact 2027 times when we book.

Official line information: Bernina Express — Rhaetian Railway.

Varenna, Lake Como — the slow middle

A small fishing village of pastel houses on the quiet eastern shore — the calm alternative to famous Bellagio, which sits just across the water. Four nights in one place, unpacked, with the lake ferries as our sightseeing.

The ferry triangle

Varenna ↔ Bellagio ↔ Menaggio by lake ferry: visit the famous villages without sleeping in their crowds. The mid-lake ferries are the region's transport and its best sightseeing.

Villas & gardens

Villa Monastero's kilometre of lakefront botanical gardens right in Varenna, and the grand showpiece Villa Carlotta across the water in Tremezzo.

Castello di Vezio

The ruined castle above the village — a short uphill walk to falconry displays and the best panorama of the lake's three branches.

Village pleasures

The Lovers' Walk along the cliffside shore, the foaming little Fiumelatte — one of Italy's shortest rivers — market mornings, a swim from the lido, perhaps a private boat hour on the lake.

At the table

Lake fish — missoltini, lavarello — and Valtellina wines from the terraced vineyards just up the valley.

Venice — three nights in the city like no other

Canals for streets, boats for buses — and crucially, nobody drives here, so every visitor moves the same way: on foot and by vaporetto. Three nights means we get Venice at its best, in the early mornings and late evenings after the day-trippers leave.

San Marco, early

The Basilica's golden mosaics (booked ahead), the Doge's Palace, and the Campanile lift for the lagoon view — all before the crowds build.

The Grand Canal at dusk

Vaporetto Line 1 down the world's greatest main street, ridden from a front seat as the palace windows light up.

Rialto mornings, bacaro evenings

The fish and produce market by day; by night, Venice's signature evening — cicchetti and small wines standing at counters, hopping bar to bar.

Get lost on purpose

The backstreets of Cannaregio and Castello, where actual Venetians live — plus the full gondola if it appeals, or the €2 traghetto crossing, the local's version.

The island day

Murano's glass furnaces and Burano — the rainbow-painted fishing village, photogenic beyond reason — by vaporetto across the lagoon.

Art & sunsets

The Accademia or the Peggy Guggenheim Collection, a La Fenice tour — and sunset on the Zattere promenade, spritz in hand.

Vaporetto passes and lines: AVM/Actv — Venice public transport. We'll buy multi-day passes on arrival.

Milan — one grand farewell

A working fashion-and-finance city with a handful of world-class sights — exactly right for one strong final evening and a calm morning before the airport express. No train-to-plane sprints on this trip.

The Duomo rooftop

Take the lift and walk among the marble spires in late-afternoon light — the single best thing to do with two hours in Milan. Official site & tickets.

Galleria & aperitivo

The 19th-century glass arcade beside the cathedral, then aperitivo with a view of it — or along the Navigli canals if energy remains.

The Last Supper, if we're lucky

Leonardo's masterpiece must be booked weeks ahead — if we secure slots, it's the best fifteen minutes in the city.

Brera farewell dinner

Cobbled lanes and galleries, and the trip's last table — or the Navigli canal-side aperitivo scene. Next morning: coffee, a short stroll, and the 50-minute Malpensa Express.

Good questions, honest answers

How much walking is involved?

Moderate, and always at our own pace. Villages like Varenna and Venice are strolling places, not hiking places — cobbles and the occasional bridge or slope (Varenna's station is a 10-minute downhill walk to the water, with taxis available for bags). Cable cars and lifts do the climbing wherever there's a view to reach, and every day has café stops built in.

Aren't all those trains tiring?

The opposite — they're the restful part. Seats are reserved on every long leg, bags stay with us (no check-ins, no carousels), there's food and coffee on board or at hand, and the two longest days are the ones with the best views. The only luggage moment of any note is one escalator-and-platform change in Milan.

What about language and money?

English works easily everywhere we're going. Cards are accepted everywhere in all three countries — no one needs to carry meaningful cash. Switzerland uses francs for our ~24 hours there, but cards cover it.

What's the weather like in September?

The sweet spot: mild and mostly settled, lakes still warm from summer, crowds thinning. Think light layers — a warm layer for the Bernina summit (2,253 m, where there's permanent snow in view) and a light rain jacket, but valley and lake days are typically shirt-sleeve weather.

Didn't Venice introduce a tourist entry fee?

Yes — for day-trippers on selected dates. Overnight guests like us are exempt; there's just a simple free pre-registration (the hotel helps with it). Nothing to worry about — we'll handle it.

What still needs to be confirmed?

Exact 2027 train times (the morning Bernina departure especially), the Lindau car-return branch and its opening hours, and seat reservations once dates are fixed. None of it changes the shape of the trip — and none of it is anyone's job but X's.

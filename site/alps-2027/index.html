How we'll travel: slowly, on purpose

We self-drive only in Bavaria; from there it's trains, lake ferries, and one scenic mountain transfer — reserved seats, bags beside us, no hop longer than half a day except the two rail days that are attractions in themselves.

Munich → Rettenberg

~2 hours by car
Details

Pick up the rental in Munich and roll south-west into the Allgäu — easy motorway, or the prettier Alpenstraße.

Rettenberg — 2–3 nights with Dr. H

Family time, village time, mountain time. The car stays with us for day trips.

Rettenberg → Lindau → Chur

~50 min drive + ~2 h train
Details

Drop the car at Lindau, then straight onto the train up the Rhine valley into Switzerland.

Chur — 1 night

An evening stroll and a good dinner in Switzerland's oldest town.

The Bernina Railway: Chur → Tirano → Iseo

Morning panoramic train + short lunch stop + ~2 h scenic drive
Details

Reserved panoramic seats over the Alps, lunch in Tirano, then a private driver over the Aprica Pass and down the Val Camonica — lakefront by mid-afternoon.

Iseo, Lago d'Iseo — 4 nights

The slow middle of the trip. Ferries, wine country, long lunches.

Iseo → Venice

~3 hours door to door
Details

A regional train through the hills to Brescia, one easy change, then the fast Frecciarossa to a station that opens directly onto the Grand Canal.

Venice — 3 nights

Early mornings and late evenings in the city, after the day-trippers leave.

Venice → Milan → home

~2½ h train, then a calm final morning
Details

No train-to-plane sprints: Milan gets its own farewell evening, then the 50-minute airport express next morning.

Booking notes (the boring bits, handled)

The car is a domestic German one-way (Munich → Lindau) — the cheap kind; panoramic-train seats get reserved the moment dates are fixed, since September fills up; the Tirano → Iseo drive is a licensed private car, booked ahead; Franciacorta cellar visits get reserved early too — September is harvest. Swiss tickets via SBB, Italian via Trenitalia. Where exact times matter — the Lindau car return, the morning train — we'll confirm them before anything is booked. Nothing here requires anyone else to plan anything.

Munich — the gentle landing

An optional first night to shake off the flights before the mountains. No sightseeing agenda.

Marienplatz & Viktualienmarkt

The old-town heart and the food market next door — a perfect first-morning wander before we pick up the car. The Glockenspiel plays at 11:00 and 12:00.

A classic beer-hall evening

Hofbräuhaus for the famous one, Augustiner Bräustuben for the local's choice. Pretzels, roast pork, litre steins.

English Garden

If energy allows: a stroll to watch the river surfers on the Eisbach wave.

Rettenberg & the Allgäu — family days in dairy country

Green foothills, onion-domed churches, and the Alps rising straight ahead — and at the centre of it, two or three days staying with Dr. H in the village.

In the village

Germany's southernmost brewery village

Rettenberg is home to Zötler — which claims to be Germany's oldest family-run brewery, brewing since 1447 — and Engelbräu. Brewery tours and beer gardens on the doorstep.

In the village

Grünten & the cheese dairies

Easy walks and viewpoints on "the guardian of the Allgäu," and alpine dairies selling Bergkäse straight from the source.

For X & E · e-bikes

The Iller valley ride

The signature day: ~50 km of flat valley path to Oberstdorf and back, mountains in view the whole way — with the Breitachklamm gorge walk or a cable car up the Nebelhorn at the turnaround. E-bikes rent locally.

For X & E · e-bikes

Lakes & valleys

A rolling lakes day (Großer Alpsee, Niedersonthofener See — still swimmable in early September), or the climb into Kleinwalsertal, a dead-end Austrian valley reachable only from Germany.

Day trip by car

Neuschwanstein & the Wieskirche

The fairy-tale castle (~1¼ h): the Marienbrücke viewpoint is the real highlight, with lunch in pretty Füssen — and the UNESCO rococo Wieskirche, standing alone in its meadow, as the natural add-on. Interior tickets get booked well in advance; the views need no ticket at all.

Day trip by car

Bregenz & the Pfänder

The cable car above Bregenz (~40 min away) for the grand Lake Constance panorama — the non-castle alternative for a day out.

Chur — one night in Switzerland's oldest town

Five thousand years of settlement, tucked into the Rhine valley. We sleep here for one clever reason: the great panoramic train leaves in the morning.

The car-free Altstadt

An evening stroll through lanes, courtyards, and squares like the Arcas — five millennia of town underfoot.

The cathedral on the hill

The 12th-century cathedral and the bishop's court district above the rooftops — a ten-minute climb, worth every step.

A Graubünden dinner

Capuns, pizzokel, Bündnerfleisch — the mountain canton's specialties, then an early night.

The Bernina Railway — the crossing

A UNESCO World Heritage line and the highest rail crossing of the Alps: glaciers, spiral viaducts and turquoise lakes, watched from a reserved panoramic seat.

Over the Alps

The Landwasser Viaduct, the Albula spirals that corkscrew inside the mountain, and the 2,253 m summit beside the milky-turquoise Lago Bianco.

Down into Italy

The balcony view from Alp Grüm, the Brusio viaduct where the train loops over itself, and lunch in Tirano — pizzoccheri, of course.

Then down to the lake

A private driver meets us in Tirano: over the Aprica Pass and down the Val Camonica, arriving on the Iseo lakefront by mid-afternoon (~2 h, scenic the whole way).

Official line information: Bernina Express — Rhaetian Railway.

Iseo, Lago d'Iseo — the slow middle

The smallest and least-touristed of the great Lombard lakes, between the Franciacorta wine hills and real mountains, with the green pyramid of Monte Isola rising from its middle. You hear Italian at dinner here. Four nights in the little lake capital — arcaded piazzas, a lakefront passeggiata — unpacked, with the ferries as our sightseeing.

Monte Isola — the highlight

A short ferry to car-free Peschiera Maraglio on Europe's largest lake island — a real fishing village, not a stage set. Bikes around the island (~9 km), or up to the Madonna della Ceriola sanctuary for the best panorama on the lake.

Franciacorta

Italy's champagne-method wine country begins directly behind town. A cellar visit — Ca' del Bosco, or Berlucchi, where the method began — and a long vineyard lunch. September is harvest season.

The ferry north

The full-lake boat past the sheer Bögn cliffs to Lovere — one of Italy's "most beautiful villages" — and Pisogne, where Romanino's fresco cycle is called the poor man's Sistine Chapel.

The Torbiere at golden hour

Boardwalks over the lily-pad lagoons of the Torbiere del Sebino reserve, just behind town — and, if we fancy a bigger outing, the prehistoric rock engravings of the Val Camonica, Italy's first UNESCO site, up the valley.

At the table

Lake fish — air-dried sardines from Monte Isola, tinca al forno with polenta — casoncelli pasta, and Franciacorta bubbles with everything.

Venice — three nights in the city like no other

Canals for streets, boats for buses — and crucially, nobody drives here, so every visitor moves the same way: on foot and by vaporetto. Three nights means we get Venice at its best, in the early mornings and late evenings after the day-trippers leave.

San Marco, early

The Basilica's golden mosaics (booked ahead), the Doge's Palace, and the Campanile lift for the lagoon view — all before the crowds build.

The Grand Canal at dusk

Vaporetto Line 1 down the world's greatest main street, ridden from a front seat as the palace windows light up.

Rialto mornings, bacaro evenings

The fish and produce market by day; by night, Venice's signature evening — cicchetti and small wines standing at counters, hopping bar to bar.

Get lost on purpose

The backstreets of Cannaregio and Castello, where actual Venetians live — plus the full gondola if it appeals, or the €2 traghetto crossing, the local's version.

The island day

Murano's glass furnaces and Burano — the rainbow-painted fishing village, photogenic beyond reason — by vaporetto across the lagoon.

Art & sunsets

The Accademia or the Peggy Guggenheim Collection, a La Fenice tour — and sunset on the Zattere promenade, spritz in hand.

Vaporetto passes and lines: AVM/Actv — Venice public transport. We'll buy multi-day passes on arrival.

Milan — one grand farewell

A working fashion-and-finance city with a handful of world-class sights — exactly right for one strong final evening and a calm morning before the airport express. No train-to-plane sprints on this trip.

The Duomo rooftop

Take the lift and walk among the marble spires in late-afternoon light — the single best thing to do with two hours in Milan. Official site & tickets.

Galleria & aperitivo

The 19th-century glass arcade beside the cathedral, then aperitivo with a view of it — or along the Navigli canals if energy remains.

The Last Supper, if we're lucky

Leonardo's masterpiece must be booked weeks ahead — if we secure slots, it's the best fifteen minutes in the city.

Brera farewell dinner

Cobbled lanes and galleries, and the trip's last table — or the Navigli canal-side aperitivo scene. Next morning: coffee, a short stroll, and the 50-minute Malpensa Express.

Good questions, honest answers

How much walking is involved?

Moderate, and always at our own pace. Iseo and Venice are strolling places, not hiking places — flat lakefront promenades, cobbles, the occasional bridge (Iseo's station is a short, level walk from the water). Cable cars and lifts do the climbing wherever there's a view to reach, and every day has café stops built in.

Aren't all those trains tiring?

The opposite — they're the restful part. Seats are reserved on every long leg, bags stay with us (no check-ins, no carousels), there's food and coffee on board or at hand, and the two longest days are the ones with the best views. The only luggage moment of any note is one easy platform change in compact Brescia station.

What about language and money?

English works easily everywhere we're going. Cards are accepted everywhere in all three countries — no one needs to carry meaningful cash. Switzerland uses francs for our ~24 hours there, but cards cover it.

What's the weather like in September?

The sweet spot: mild and mostly settled, lakes still warm from summer, crowds thinning. Think light layers — a warm layer for the Bernina summit (2,253 m, where there's permanent snow in view) and a light rain jacket, but valley and lake days are typically shirt-sleeve weather.

Didn't Venice introduce a tourist entry fee?

Yes — for day-trippers on selected dates. Overnight guests like us are exempt; there's just a simple free pre-registration (the hotel helps with it). Nothing to worry about — we'll handle it.

What still needs to be confirmed?

Exact 2027 train times (the morning Bernina departure especially), the Lindau car-return branch and its opening hours, the Tirano → Iseo driver, and seat reservations once dates are fixed. None of it changes the shape of the trip — and none of it is anyone's job but X's.
